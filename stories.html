Programming & Software

Ada Lovelace

10 December 1815 - 27 November 1852

Mathematician

Wrote an algorithm for Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine, widely considered the first published computer program.

Ada Lovelace was an English mathematician who is often described as the world's first computer programmer. While working with Charles Babbage's plans for the Analytical Engine, she recognised that the machine could be used for more than mathematical calculations. Her ideas about programming and the potential of computers were far ahead of her time.

Grace Hopper

9 December 1906 - 1 January 1992

Computer Scientist, Mathematician, U.S. Navy Rear Admiral

Helped develop COBOL and pioneered compiler technology.

Grace Hopper was an American computer scientist who played an important role in making programming more accessible. She helped develop compilers, which translate human-written instructions into a form computers can understand, and contributed to the development of COBOL. Her work influenced how modern programming languages are designed and used.

Margaret Hamilton

17 August 1936 - Present

Computer Scientist, Software Engineer

Led the development of the Apollo spacecraft's onboard flight software.

Margaret Hamilton is an American computer scientist and software engineer who worked on NASA's Apollo missions. She led the team responsible for developing the software that helped guide the Apollo spacecraft. Her work demonstrated the importance of reliable software in critical situations and helped establish software engineering as an important field.

NASA & Space Technology

Katherine Johnson

26 August 1918 - 24 February 2020

Mathematician, NASA Research Mathematician

Calculated flight trajectories for NASA's early human spaceflight missions.

Katherine Johnson was an American mathematician who worked for NASA and its predecessor, NACA. She performed important calculations for missions including John Glenn's orbital flight and the Apollo missions. Her mathematical work helped NASA accurately plan spacecraft trajectories and contributed to the success of America's early space programme.

Dorothy Vaughan

20 September 1910 - 10 November 2008

Mathematician, Computer Programmer, NASA Supervisor

Became an expert in FORTRAN programming and helped NASA transition from human calculations to electronic computing.

Dorothy Vaughan was an American mathematician and programmer who worked at NASA's Langley Research Center. She initially performed complex calculations as a human computer before learning FORTRAN programming. She became a specialist in electronic computing and helped prepare her team for the growing use of computers in NASA's research.

Annie Easley

23 April 1933 - 25 June 2011

Computer Scientist, Mathematician, Rocket Scientist

Developed computer programs for NASA's rocket, energy and propulsion research.

Annie Easley was an American computer scientist and mathematician who worked at NASA's Lewis Research Center. She began her career performing calculations by hand before learning computer programming. She developed software for energy research and worked on projects involving rocket propulsion and alternative energy technologies.

Science & Engineering

Mary Jackson

9 April 1921 - 11 February 2005

Mathematician, Aerospace Engineer

Became NASA's first Black female engineer and conducted research into aerodynamics.

Mary Jackson was an American mathematician and aerospace engineer who worked at NASA's Langley Research Center. She conducted research into how air moves around aircraft and contributed to aerospace engineering projects. Later in her career, she worked to improve opportunities for women and minorities within NASA.

Hedy Lamarr

9 September 1914 - 19 January 2000

Inventor, Actress

Co-invented a frequency-hopping communication system that contributed to the development of spread-spectrum technology.

Hedy Lamarr was an Austrian-born American inventor and actress. During World War II, she and composer George Antheil developed a frequency-hopping communication system intended to prevent radio-controlled signals from being easily disrupted. Although it was not immediately used for its original purpose, the underlying technology later became important to the development of wireless communication.

Radia Perlman

18 December 1951 - Present

Computer Scientist, Network Engineer

Invented the Spanning Tree Protocol (STP), which helps computer networks avoid connection loops.

Radia Perlman is an American computer scientist and network engineer who made major contributions to computer networking. She created the Spanning Tree Protocol, which allows interconnected computers and network devices to communicate efficiently without creating loops. Her work has helped make large computer networks more reliable and organised.
